# AVF — Allowed Visual Families (Universal Reference for Oral-Care Products)

> **Purpose.** This is the master reference for how active ingredients, mechanisms, and natural compounds may be depicted in any Das Experten oral-care visual — banners, slides, product cards, infographics, fact-sheet graphics. Every microscopic / molecular / mechanism-of-action visual must come from one of the families below. If a desired visual is not described here, it is not approved — escalate before generating.

> **Scope gate (read first).**
> - **Family A (Molecular & Biochemical)** → permitted **ONLY for toothpastes & liquids** (pastes, gels, mouthwashes, serums).
> - **Family B (Phytochemical & Natural Compound)** → **universal** — permitted for **all** oral-care categories, including accessories (brushes, floss, etc.).

---

## A. Molecular & Biochemical Structures — TOOTHPASTES & LIQUIDS ONLY

Focuses on the action mechanisms of active ingredients at the nanoscale, emphasizing efficacy and scientific backing.

### A1. Molecular Clusters & Architectures
Complex molecular arrangements emphasizing spatial relationships and interactive surfaces of core components.

- **Polymeric structures** — the scaffolding and network formed by long-chain molecules within the product matrix or on the tooth surface.
- **Micellar envelopes** — spherical arrangement of surfactant or lipid molecules that encapsulate and solubilize impurities or active compounds.
- **Crystalline lattices** — ordered, repeating structure of mineral components or drug-delivery systems; sharp, geometric precision.

### A2. Enzymes / Peptides / Polyphenols — Catalytic & Protective Mechanisms
High-resolution visualizations of specific enzymatic, peptidic and phenolic reactions.

- **Dextranase action — polysaccharide-cleaving arcs.** Active site docking onto and hydrolyzing the glucan chains (dextrans) forming the plaque biofilm backbone, resulting in structural breakdown and disaggregation of the plaque layer.
- **Invertase function — sucrose-splitting micro-reaction chains.** Enzyme catalyzing breakdown of dietary sucrose into less-harmful monosaccharides (glucose + fructose) directly in the oral cavity, reducing the local fermentable-sugar load available to cariogenic bacteria.
- **GOX (Glucose Oxidase) system — hydrogen-peroxide micro-generation nodes + antimicrobial gradient halos.** Controlled localized conversion of glucose into gluconic acid and trace H₂O₂; visual highlights a low-concentration but effective antimicrobial gradient field that selectively inhibits oral pathogens without irritating surrounding tissue.
- **Papain activity — proteolytic softening pathways.** Cysteine protease cleaving peptide bonds within the denatured protein pellicle and food-debris layers on enamel, softening surface stains and organic build-up for easier mechanical removal.
- **Bromelain mechanism — protease lattices fragmenting organic deposits.** Pineapple-derived broad-spectrum proteolytic complex systematically breaking down complex organic macromolecules in surface deposits; visually distinct from papain. Aids deep cleansing and polish.
- **Lysozyme action — peptidoglycan-disrupting vectors.** Glycosidase precision attack on the β-(1→4) glycosidic linkages in bacterial peptidoglycan cell walls, leading to osmotic lysis and disruption of bacterial integrity — a key natural defense vector.

### A3. Bioactive Organic Systems
Broader category for natural extracts/compounds best shown as a system or complex interaction rather than a single enzymatic event.

### A4. Microbiome Lattices & Probiotic Interactions
- **Bacillus coagulans — spore-forming probiotic spheres, microbiome-balancing nodes, pathogen-downregulation lattice.** Resilient heat-stable spores germinating in the oral environment to establish competitive advantage; beneficial bacteria form a dense protective lattice that modulates local pH and nutrient availability, downregulating colonization and virulence of common oral pathogens.
- **Antimicrobial / probiotic interactions.** Probiotics out-competing harmful bacteria for binding sites on the oral mucosa while producing bacteriocins/metabolites that create an unfavorable environment for pathogens.

### A5. Ion-Exchange / Remineralization Schematics
- **Recaldent (CPP-ACP) system — casein-phosphopeptide nanocomplexes, amorphous calcium-phosphate delivery nodes, enamel-binding remineralization halos.** CPP carrier binds the tooth surface and releases bioavailable Amorphous Calcium Phosphate (ACP); ACP diffuses into subsurface lesions and crystallizes into new acid-resistant hydroxyapatite, forming a protective remineralization halo around compromised enamel.
- **pH-modulating networks.** Buffering agents and alkaline compounds dynamically neutralizing acid challenges in plaque and saliva, maintaining a stable oral pH critical to preventing demineralization.

## B. Phytochemical & Natural Compound Structures — UNIVERSAL (All Oral-Care Products)

Depictions of nature-derived actives, emphasizing characteristic molecular shape and functional mechanism.

- **Clove (Eugenol) — amber phenolic rings, antimicrobial binding sites.** Distinct ring structure inserting into and disrupting bacterial membranes; vibrant amber-hued molecule interacting with a cell-wall matrix.
- **Cinnamon (Cinnamaldehyde) — elongated aldehyde chains, anti-biofilm suppression lines.** Linear unsaturated structure interfering with quorum sensing (bacterial communication), suppressing development of dense protective plaque layers.
- **Hemp Seed Oil — omega lipid arcs, green–gold membrane hydration halos.** Omega fatty acids as flowing resilient lipid arcs fortifying and soothing mucosal cell membranes; subtle protective hydration halo in green/gold tones.
- **Coconut Oil (lauric acid focus) — micelle clusters, soft-white antimicrobial lipid spheres.** Medium-chain triglycerides (esp. lauric acid) forming spherical micelle clusters with broad-spectrum antimicrobial action by dissolving the lipid layer of microbial membranes.
- **Ginger Extract (Gingerol / Shogaol) — angular phenolic lattices, anti-inflammatory downregulation arcs.** Sharp characteristic chemical structure; modulates inflammatory pathways (e.g. COX-2 inhibition) to reduce gum redness/swelling, shown as an inhibitory arc.
- **Activated Charcoal (coconut-shell derived) — ultra-porous micro-honeycomb lattice, high-surface adsorption zones, deeper toxin binding vs. hardwood charcoal.** SEM-style visualization of the highly organized ultra-porous structure; contrast emphasizes vast internal surface area and deeper pore structure superior to less-refined hardwood charcoal — a high-capacity adsorption medium for surface stains, toxins, and odors.

## Usage Rules

1. **Pick the family by product type first.** Liquids/pastes → A or B. Brushes/accessories → B only.
2. **One mechanism per visual.** Do not blend two enzyme actions into a single illustration unless explicitly briefed.
3. **Stay literal to the mechanism described.** The arc/lattice/halo language above is the approved visual vocabulary — reuse those exact metaphors.
4. **Brand palette still applies** (white/silver + turquoise/blue per the technolog defaults). AVF governs *what* is depicted; brand guidelines govern *how* it is colored.
5. **Not listed = not approved.** If a brief asks for a mechanism outside this file, flag it before generating.
